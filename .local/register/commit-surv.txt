Getting somebody out, and earning what comes against you (0.8.3-dev)

Two loose ends closed, both named honestly in the previous checkpoint rather than
buried.

Recruiting a survivor. The previous checkpoint placed them -- alive, neutral,
carryable out -- and said plainly that without a way to accept them a survivor
was scenery you could pick up rather than a person you could save. This is
offering passage rather than recruiting: no negotiation, no recruitment chance
and no prisoner step, because somebody lost in the Backrooms who meets a team
with a way out wants to leave, and making a player roll for that would be a worse
story and a worse game.

The structurally important part is that joining is also what lets them leave. The
traversal rule is absolute -- an inhabitant may never decide anything about a
gate -- so a survivor who has not joined cannot cross, and the only way out for
them is to be carried, exactly like anything else found down there. Joining makes
them a colonist and PortalTraversalPolicy then permits it through the same single
chokepoint everything else uses. Nothing here special-cases a gate, deliberately:
the rule is enforced in one place and this is one more caller obeying it rather
than an exception to it. The letter says so out loud, because it is the most
interesting thing about the interaction.

The comp sits on the human race def so every pawn in the game carries it, and it
does nothing unless a coordinate marked that particular person as a survivor it
produced -- the same dormant-until-designated pattern the gate, emergence and
credit beacon comps use on Core buildings. One of the branch's own people must be
present to make the offer, so a survivor cannot be recruited from the other side
of a gate by a player looking at a map.

The encounter cap as a recorded progression step. This was the last unmet clause
of the original ladder direction from 2026-09-28: caps where raising a cap is
itself a recorded progression step. The cap and the absolute ceiling were built
in 0.8.0-dev; what was missing is that the cap should not simply be the ceiling
from day one. It has to be earned, recorded and visible in the branch's own
history, so a player can look back and see the moment the rules changed rather
than discovering that the world quietly got harder.

The step is reaching a depth nobody has reached before, and that choice is
deliberate. Wealth already feeds the ladder's ceiling, so using it again here
would double-count one input, and research is not an act of exploration. Pushing
deeper than the branch has ever been is the one thing that is unambiguously the
player choosing to escalate, which means the Backrooms never brings more against
somebody than they went looking for. A branch that stays shallow stays at the
opening cap forever however rich or advanced it becomes, and that is the point
rather than a side effect.

The cap opens at one so a first dangerous space is dangerous rather than
overwhelming, rises by one per new deepest coordinate, and never passes the
absolute ceiling of three that keeps the solo-survivability condition true. It is
idempotent, so a cap cannot be walked up by re-entering one space, and depth one
never raises anything because every branch starts there. A step that hits the
ceiling is still recorded, because the history should show the branch went deeper
even when nothing changed.
