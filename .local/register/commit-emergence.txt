A way out of the Backrooms, and it comes up where you said (0.6.9-dev)

The half of the topology direction that was never built. Everything assumed the
far endpoint of a portal was a branch-owned coordinate: RegisterNaturalAddress
took a CoordinateRecord and EnsureSite generated a Backrooms map for it. This is
the bounded form the previous record named, where the far side is an ordinary
map the branch already holds, and it is what makes the owner's own examples
map > backrooms > map > backrooms and backrooms > map > backrooms > backrooms >
map route end to end.

One orientation choice made most of it free. An emergence edge is recorded
anchor-first: First is the marked door on the ordinary branch-owned map, Second
is the doorway inside the coordinate. That is the same orientation every other
kind already uses, so Availability needed no change, Register's site check on the
second anchor needed no change, and the uniqueness rule needed no change. What
the kind adds is a single gate in Register, mirroring line for line the check
laboratories already get on CompRimroomsGate. The enum value is appended and
never renumbered, and the three places testing "is this kind known" became one
helper rather than a fourth copy of the condition.

The player marks where a way out comes up, and nothing ever picks for them.
CompRimroomsEmergence sits on Core Door and Autodoor, added by one additive
patch beside the existing gate comp, dormant until marked. This inherits
0.6.3-dev's rule with more force rather than less, because a way out arrives at
the player's own map: a door somebody built is never quietly turned into a hole
in the world. Marking is refused inside the Backrooms, and the command is not
even offered there, because a way out cannot come up in the place it leads away
from. Withdrawing a mark deliberately leaves an existing way out alone -- it
means "no more ways out here", not "close the one that exists", because a saved
edge is evidence of a place somebody found.

A doorway that already leads onward gets a second, independent draw deciding
deeper or out, with a distinct seed key so the two can never correlate, derived
from the coordinate's own seed and the doorway's position so it is stable across
saves and revisits. One in three leads out, deliberately common, because a way
home is what makes the rest of the topology usable rather than a trap. With
nothing marked the doorway leads deeper instead -- a fallback, not a refusal.
The question is asked before the coordinate is minted, because minting one and
then not using it would leave a space nobody can reach recorded against the
branch.

Two unrelated defects found on the way. RR_Gate_OperatorAway was declared twice
in one file with two different texts, serving both a readout that takes the
operator's name as {0} and a refusal with no argument. RimWorld resolves
duplicates last-one-wins, so one of the two messages was always wrong. The
refusal now has its own key matching its own text. And nothing was checking for
it: tools/check-keyed-strings.py now verifies no duplicate keys, every literal
RR_ reference resolving, and format arguments lining up. It resolves against the
mod's own declared defNames and against internal identifiers recognised by the
shape of the call site rather than a maintained list, for the same reason the DLC
gating check reads the game's data -- a list of names rots exactly the way the
thing it checks rots. 1,114 keys, 0 duplicates, 1,086 references all resolving.

Now genuinely live rather than hypothetical: Area_BuildRoof, Area_NoRoof,
Area_SnowOrSandClear and Area_PollutionClear have no cross-gate route, and an
ordinary map reachable through a gate gets snow, wants roofs built and may be
polluted. The zone audit recorded them as a dependency of exactly this endpoint.

The owner's kill switch request arrived while this was in flight. It is recorded
verbatim in TODO.md and deliberately not built here: this feature was half
finished and compiling, and leaving it that way to start something else would
have left an inconsistent tree.
