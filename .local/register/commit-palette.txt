The yellow rooms, and what happens further in (0.7.8-dev)

The first Backrooms space now looks like the Backrooms: yellow carpet, yellow
wood walls, and lights mounted on the walls rather than lamps standing in the
middle of the floor.

The owner asked for that look and, in the next message, said it is only the
shallow one -- further in it gets very varied and weird. That second message is
what shaped the design, because a single global palette could only ever deliver
half of it. So the look became a function of depth, which meant adding a depth
concept the project did not have: a coordinate now records how far in it sits,
counted in portals from the ordinary world. A frontier on a world map mints depth
one and every step inward adds one, without limit.

Depth one is fixed and never rolls. The first space a player ever sees must be
the yellow rooms, on every seed, every time; it is the image the whole setting
rests on and a random alternative there would be a worse game for the sake of
variety. Deeper bands roll from the coordinate's own seed, so a space is stable
across reloads and two spaces at the same depth are not identical. Five bands:
poolrooms, machinery, abandoned offices, cold storage, and one where the palette
stops agreeing with itself.

Core already had the light. The owner reached for floor lights on the assumption
the game had no overhead fluorescent, and it ships WallLamp. That is the better
answer twice over: closer to the intended look, and it frees the floor, because
an endless corridor reads as endless precisely when nothing is standing in it.
Generation had been using StandingLamp, which put a piece of furniture in the
middle of every room.

The yellow itself is Core content wearing a colour. The floor is Core Carpet,
whose own description says it is dyed a single colour, and TerrainGrid.colorGrid
is a public field, so the tint is applied directly after SetTerrain with no
Harmony and no new terrain def. Walls are Core Wall built from wood and tinted
through CompColorable, the same mechanism generation already used for its grey
and tan rooms. The tint is a sickly mustard rather than a clean primary, which is
what the reference actually looks like. No new texture, no new terrain, no new
building.

One Core behaviour worth recording: SetTerrain clears the colour grid, so the
colour has to be applied after the terrain and the cell redrawn. Missing either
produces a floor that is silently the wrong colour until something else dirties
the mesh.

The band list is deliberately short. Variation deeper down should come from what
is in a room rather than from an ever-growing list of paint schemes, because a
palette that never repeats stops reading as a place at all. That is where the
owner's labs, workshops, nurseries and everything imaginable belongs, and it is
the next body of work rather than this one.
