Odd supply contracts: somebody who actually wants the goods (0.7.3-dev)

0.7.2-dev built the marker. Without a buyer, "(odd)" is a label nobody reads.
This is the half that makes it an economy, and it answers the rest of the owner's
direction: quests and missions and contracts for 1000 (odd) cotton or 10
uninstalled electric stoves (odd).

One decision shaped everything else. The tempting implementation is to pick any
thing definition in the game and demand a pile of it, and it produces contracts a
player cannot possibly fill: a thousand odd cotton is unanswerable if no space
the branch has ever opened contained cotton, and the player would hunt for hours
and then correctly conclude the feature is broken. So every coordinate records
the distinct definitions it really produced, once, at generation, and a demand is
only ever drawn from the union of those records.

That turned out to be a better shape than the naive one rather than merely a
safer one. Exploration now drives demand instead of demand arriving from nowhere:
open a space, see what it holds, and buyers appear who want it.

Bounded, deterministic and saved. Three open at once, so a long campaign cannot
accumulate a backlog nobody will ever fill. About a day between offers. The
choice of goods derives from the campaign seed and a saved counter and never from
Rand, so reloading a save cannot reroll a hard demand into an easy one. Quantity
scales by the thing's own stackLimit, which is how "10 uninstalled electric
stoves" is expressed in the units the game already uses. Payment comes from the
thing's own BaseMarketValue times six, clamped, so it tracks the game's economy
and any mod that changes a value rather than a table this mod would have to
maintain against 294 other mods. The multiplier is there because the buyer cannot
source these anywhere else and the player paid in gate time, power and risk
rather than in silver.

Payment happens before the goods are consumed, deliberately. It is the idempotent
step, so a save reloaded mid-delivery reports already-applied rather than paying
twice, and the goods are consumed exactly once either way.

Matching reads through OddOriginService.IsOdd, which resolves a MinifiedThing to
its InnerThing. That is the owner's own stove example and the case that would
silently never settle if the wrapper were asked directly.

One integrity rule had to change and it is worth stating rather than burying. The
campaign component validated that every contract names a coordinate that exists.
An odd-supply contract is branch-wide -- it buys goods by origin, and any
coordinate that produced them satisfies it -- so it legitimately carries no
coordinate id, and leaving the old rule in place would have faulted a perfectly
valid save. The check now asks the right question of each kind rather than the
same question of both.

A coordinate saved before 0.7.2-dev has no recorded odd goods, and an empty list
is the honest answer: it offers no supply contracts of its own rather than having
contents invented for a space generated before any of this existed.
