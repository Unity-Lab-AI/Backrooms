The only clock is the gate (0.11.0-dev)

A stop-building instruction, obeyed. No quest, request or research
content was written. What shipped is the campaign chart, the two
absolutes made enforceable, and the removal of the two clocks that
already broke one of them.

The part that went wrong, recorded first. The clock removals were made
by deleting a tuned def field, a const and a keyed string outright with
no archive, and the owner stopped it. Two failures, the second worse.
Whether "offeres and trades" covered a hiring applicant and a purchase
quote was a fork with two readings, and it was guessed rather than
asked. And retired content is archived, never deleted -- an existing
invariant followed for the survey tag, the evidence case and the machine
gate, skipped here, so a tuned 420000 and a 25000 went into a diff and
nowhere else. Nothing had been committed, so the tree was restored to
3cf7dc8 intact, build and checkers re-verified, and the scope asked. The
owner confirmed the reading, which means the conclusion was right and
the method was wrong -- the more dangerous shape, because it looks like
progress. The redo archives every value, field, call site and string
before removing anything.

Two clocks retired, neither of which bounded anything. A hiring offer
withdrew itself after seven in-game days; a purchase quote expired after
about ten in-game hours. Both pools were already capped by a count, with
new requests refused at the cap, so both clocks were pure pressure with
no function. refreshTicks, the same number on the same def, is kept
because a cooldown cannot be failed. Both expiresTick fields and the
Expired enum members stay scribed and unread, because dropping a scribed
field to tidy up is how a saved game stops loading.

Seven prep documents promised the opposite, which is the argument for
doing the chart before the content. Since Gate 0 the prep material had
promised deadlines, timed distortion investigations and consequences for
delay. Nothing was ever built from those lines, so correcting the
blueprint cost nothing; written a week later it would have cost a
rewrite. Eleven documents corrected. Two hits deliberately left because
they argue for the rule. "Consequence for abandonment" survives
everywhere: abandoning is an act, being slow is not.

check-campaign-absolutes.py is the eighth checker. No source identifier
named as an expiry outside a five-entry allowlist with reasons, no def
field, no living document promising one, and every offer def carrying
two or more success routes -- of which zero exist, stated plainly, so
the first offer ever written has to satisfy it. Deliberately narrow: it
does not scan every ...Ticks field, because forty of them are delays and
cooldowns and flagging those makes a checker people scroll past. It
found five documents the hand sweep had missed, and it caught two more
in this checkpoint's own NOW.md and FINALIZED.md. A negation was briefly
widened with "banned" and "superseded" so existing text would pass, and
taken straight back out.

docs/CAMPAIGN_CHART.md is the authority on campaign structure and wins
over any prep document: the two absolutes, the tutorial-to-campaign
hinge, eight arcs with what is built, five research tiers and nine
branches, the mission line with its routes, the corporation's character,
what the prep got wrong, three decisions named as the owner's, and the
build order. The gate branch is the only complete one of nine.

160 C# files, 83 package files, zero warnings. Eight checkers pass, both
proofs hold, assembly reproduced by two clean recompiles. No content
built, deliberately. No game launched.
