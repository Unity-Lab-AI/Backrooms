Company bonds, from ten credits to a quadrillion (0.7.5-dev)

Corporate credits can now be held as physical bearer paper, printed at a bench,
hauled, stored in a vault, banked at a beacon, and lost in a fire.

Three layers of money, with the bond as the bridge. Silver, gold, jade and ivory
are entirely untouched vanilla and a vault works as it always did. The company
account stays the abstract ledger: audited, weightless, impossible to steal
because it is not anywhere. The bond is the only one of the three that can burn,
and per the owner's answer that physical means physical, that is exactly the
trade-off which makes the account still worth having. Liquidity costs risk.

The ladder runs to ten to the fifteenth rather than to a million, following the
owner's immediate follow-up not to stop at a million. Stopping there would have
needed a million pieces of paper to represent one trillion, against a parent
corporation recorded at multi-trillion scale. Fifteen rungs keeps that to three
or four bonds and stays far inside long, whose ceiling is about 9.2e18, so even
thousands of top bonds cannot overflow a total.

Every rung is exactly ten of the one below, which is what keeps the chain simple
and is also why the payout rule is provably correct: greedy largest-first is
optimal precisely because every rung is an exact multiple of every rung beneath
it. That is why a strict power-of-ten ladder was worth insisting on rather than
something like one-five-ten. Anything under ten credits stays in the account
rather than being rounded away, so a player's money is never lost to the shape of
the ladder.

The bench bills produce nothing, and that is the interesting part. A bond's value
lives per instance in a comp, a recipe's products list can only name a ThingDef
and a count, and the hook that post-processes a finished product is private and
static. There is no supported way to stamp a value onto something a bill just
made, and the workaround would be Harmony. So the worker mints the bond itself in
Notify_IterationCompleted, which is a documented virtual. Two things fall out and
both are better than the original plan: the denomination comes from the recipe
that is running rather than from guessing which object on the floor was the one
just made, and an unfunded print produces nothing at all instead of an unstamped
book worth free market value. Adding a rung needs one more RecipeDef and no code,
because the denomination is parsed from the recipe's own name and the ladder
stays the source of truth.

The face value is debited before the bond is made and refunded if the paper
cannot be placed. Printing paper the company cannot back is printing money, which
is the one thing this layer exists to prevent.

The credit beacon was the owner's own suggestion and the best reuse in the batch.
A Core orbital trade beacon already means that the valuables inside this circle
are the ones that count, so nobody has to learn a new idea and Core already draws
the radius on the ground. Dormant until designated, exactly like the gate comps
on Core doors, so an unrelated beacon in somebody's existing colony behaves as it
always has. Banking destroys the paper and credits the account in one transaction
for the lot -- counted, then destroyed, then posted -- so a reload mid-action
cannot credit a subset twice. That is the requirement that redeeming must not
leave a dead item: no spent certificate, no zero-value object to haul away.

Repurposed rather than invented: the bond is a Core Novel carrying a comp, which
is what a bearer instrument physically is. No new item and no new texture, and an
unstamped Novel is still an ordinary novel. Bonds are stamped Outside on creation
so one can never be sold as odd goods. AllowStackWith keeps face values apart
even though Novel has a stack limit of one today, because that is a fact about a
Core def rather than a guarantee, and a mod raising a book's stack limit would
otherwise silently merge two bonds and destroy the quieter one.
