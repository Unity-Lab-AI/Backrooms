Every surface the game speaks through (0.10.5-dev)

RimWorld does not have one voice for displayed text. It has a different
convention per surface, and Core is consistent about them to a degree
that is easy to violate without noticing: a float menu row ends with a
full stop 7% of the time, an explanatory tooltip 93% of the time. Core's
own keyed files are organised by surface where ours are organised by
system, and the conventions were lost in the gap.

Six strings were written in the wrong register. Five right-click rows
were sentences; one of them was a 98-character row carrying a
two-sentence instruction. The row now states only the condition and the
instruction moved to the button's tooltip, which is the surface that
exists for instructions.

check-display-style.py is the seventh checker. It classifies each key by
the call site that displays it rather than by its name, because a name
does not say where a string appears, and it prints a census of every
surface RimWorld displays text on including the ones at zero. That
census is how the alerts readout was found empty: every warning this mod
gave was either a letter a player can dismiss and lose, or an inspect
line a player has to already be looking at.

Three alerts now. Recovery overdue at critical, return window closing at
high and still actionable, no gate operator at medium and colony-wide so
that keeping a spare door designated does not nag. No def, no asset, no
Harmony: AlertsReadout walks typeof(Alert).AllLeafSubclasses(), verified
by decompiling it.

Register row 146 changed the design. It was the only applicable row in
295, and its review carries the author's own note about a possible small
alert-check cost. Core calls GetReport on a rotating one-in-twenty-four
schedule, so three alerts would mean three building sweeps forever. They
share one cached sweep per game tick, keyed on the tick and the game
object, because keying on the tick alone hands a second save loaded at
the same tick the first save's despawned components.

The checker's first run reported seven faults that were not faults, and
the baselines were wrong rather than the text. Letter bodies had been
measured from Letters.xml alone at a maximum of 385 when Core's incident
letters reach 666; tooltips from GameplayCommands.xml alone at 204 when
Core's explanatory strings reach 894. One good tooltip was flagged for
being four characters over a ceiling that never existed. Measure a
surface, never a file. Both rules were then sanity-tested by planting a
fault on each and confirming it fired.

The inspect pane is counted and deliberately not ruled on, and the report
says so in words: Core builds inspect lines from strings scattered across
its keyed files and there is no clean population to measure against.

160 C# files, 80 package files, zero warnings. Seven checkers pass.
Assembly reproduced by two clean recompiles. No game launched.
