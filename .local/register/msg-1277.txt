Every battery counts, and the refusal tells you the truth (0.12.77-dev)

The owner reported from a running game that only one battery could be connected
to a gate and that it was not enough, and guessed it was a power problem. Both
halves were right.

The bound battery was only ever the anchor that identifies which power net is the
gate's circuit. NativeGenerationWatts has always summed the whole net and
NativePowerConnected has always checked it; stored energy was the one reading that
never followed, so every other battery on the circuit counted for nothing. The
spend was worse than the reading: TrySpendNativeEnergy refused outright when the
anchor alone could not cover a cost, which stalled a gate that had ten full
batteries beside it on the same net.

Fixed through Core's own mechanisms. PowerNet.CurrentStoredEnergy() does the sum
and skips EMP-stunned batteries for free. The draw is copied from
ChangeStoredEnergy, which does exactly this and is private, so the gate treats
batteries the way the game treats its own. The anchor is still required -
removing the limit is not removing the binding.

And the message had sent the owner the wrong way. HasUsablePortalWindow collapsed
seven conditions into one bool, the network turned false into Closed, and the
player read "The laboratory connection for that address is not open." A flat
battery reported an address fault, which is why a session went into looking at the
address. PortalWindowBlockerKey is the third blocker key in this file's history
after CalibrationBlockerKey and StaffConsoleBlockerKey, both added because one
generic refusal covered several problems with several different fixes.
HasUsablePortalWindow delegates to it now, so the predicate and the message cannot
disagree.

returnReserveCapacityWattDays is 2 and a Core Battery holds 600, so the bind-time
ReserveTooSmall refusal was checked and ruled out rather than assumed. And nothing
in forty-eight proofs had ever claimed anything about the energy a gate runs on,
which is why a defect this central reached play.

The public documentation is a wiki now: thirteen short pages covering install, mod
manager setup, the first hour, gates, what is through them, the company, the
interface, mods, multiplayer and troubleshooting, with clearly-marked placeholders
for the Workshop links. The reader-facing list it replaces held thirteen entries
and most were never reader documents - one opens "the practical guide for anyone
(human or build agent) opening this repository" and another calls itself a design
contract with tuning hypotheses. Holding a development document to a reader's
vocabulary made it look supervised while nothing was going to notice it was the
wrong kind of document. The wall limit is 360 where it was 700; the wiki tops out
at 307.

Yesterday's dependency decision made "Core only" false in twenty-six living
documents, so a rule now reads the declared count out of About.xml and refuses any
living document claiming there are none. If the decision is ever reversed the rule
stops firing on its own. The verbatim ledger is exempt and the reason is a LAW:
those four files carry the owner's recorded words and LAW #0 forbids altering them.

The plants caught five consequences of this session's own work. Three went MISSED
because the play document left the supervised set, which is the one thing a
reader-facing rule set loses silently. One was only ever passing because of a bug -
it planted a stat base inside an XML comment, and that checker strips comments
since the fix for reading its own prose. And one claim was the duplicate-string
trap for the third time in a session, asserting a line that appears twice, so
gutting one copy left the claim true.

The new dependency rule also reported wrong line numbers on its first run, because
it enumerated stripped text while reporting file positions. A finding with the
wrong address is worse than no finding.
