Origin completeness, and the weight of being somewhere wrong (0.7.4-dev)

Two systems from one owner direction: finish the odd-origin model, and add the
psychological layer that makes the odd/ordinary split matter beyond selling.

The owner floated marking things at the gate and immediately doubted it -- "maybe
mark it at the gate but idk". The doubt was right, so it was not built. Gate
marking is a laundering route: carry ordinary cotton in, carry it out, it is now
odd. And it fails the owner's very next clause, "it should be odd when in the
backrooms too", because nothing would be odd until it crossed.

What generation-time marking really missed was everything meant by "everything in
it entirety": rock mined out of a coordinate's walls, material from a
deconstructed partition, plants cut in its rooms, meat butchered from something
found there. None of that is generated content.

So a three-state origin replaces the boolean -- Unknown, Backrooms, Outside --
stamped the first time a thing exists anywhere. That closes the laundering route
by construction rather than by a rule: by the time a colonist hauls cotton
through a gate it was stamped Outside back in the colony and can never become
odd, which means anything appearing on a Backrooms map still Unknown genuinely
came into existence there. Mined rock, deconstruction returns, cut plants and
butchered meat all become odd with no special case for any of them.

One seam is named rather than fought. Haul ordinary steel in, build a wall,
deconstruct it, and the returns are odd -- but deconstruction refunds about half,
so the cycle loses material every time and is economically irrational.

The pressure is the piece that ties the mod together. Until now odd versus
ordinary was purely economic, and this makes it psychological: it gives the
player a reason to carry ordinary material into a coordinate rather than only
carrying odd material out. The tension is real, because every ordinary thing
hauled in to make the place bearable is a thing that was not sold. It also
finally gives the forward base a purpose -- thirty-one work families could
already work across a gate, and this is what makes it worth building somewhere
for them to do it from.

Saved as ticks per person rather than a thought with a timer, so the penalty
decays on leaving instead of snapping back. An hour down there follows somebody
home, which forces shift rotation instead of one colonist living there forever.
Minus one on arrival as a floor nothing removes; minus ten at 216,000 ticks,
which is one real hour at normal speed and exactly double the gate's opening
window, so both systems measure time in the same unit. Recovery runs at double
the accumulation rate.

Shelter is scored from the pawn's actual surroundings rather than a research
unlock or a stat. A stat would have been easier and would have meant nothing; the
only honest reading of "real materials in their surroundings" is to go and look.
Ordinary-origin construction weighs most, then an enclosed room, then ordinary
seating or a bed, then light. The best a player can build slows accumulation to a
fifth and never to zero, because a perfectly appointed room in a coordinate is
still a room in a coordinate. Fixtures found in place score nothing, which is the
entire point.

Applied to the player's people and anyone they carried in, not to generated
inhabitants. The owner said "all pawns"; the honest reading is everybody who does
not belong there, since a native is not unsettled by its own home and nothing
reads its mood anyway.
