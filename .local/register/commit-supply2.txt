The parent corporation as a trader, behind three locks (0.7.6-dev)

An orbital trader called in from the gate console, whose catalogue is tiered and
whose tiers are locked behind research, completed company work, and a credit
access fee.

Three locks, and each gates a different kind of progress, which is why none of
them is redundant. Research means you understand it. A completed company contract
means you have done something for them. A credit fee means you paid for the
privilege rather than only for the goods.

The credit lock is the structurally important one: it is what makes the bond
layer worth anything beyond storage. Before this, a vault of paper was a place to
keep money; now it buys capability.

The contract lock reuses the existing contract record rather than inventing a
parallel quest system, so an odd-goods supply contract or the onboarding survey
is the thing that earns a tier. The whole economy keeps pointing at one loop
instead of growing a second one beside it.

The first tier has no locks at all, on purpose. A corporation that sells you
nothing until you have already succeeded is not a supplier, it is a wall.

Universal by category rather than by hand-listed item, as asked. Each tier names
a ThingCategoryDef, so it sells whatever the loaded game puts in that category --
Core, DLC and any of the other 274 mods alike. This mod lists no items and
invents none, and a profile that adds new metals sells new metals here the day it
is installed.

Locked means invisible, and the second half of that is easy to miss. A locked
tier yields no stock and also reports that it handles none of its own
definitions, because HandlesThingDef is what decides whether a trader will buy a
thing. A tier that generated nothing but still claimed its category would quietly
let a player sell into a catalogue they had not unlocked.

Not a subclass of Core's category generator, and that was checked rather than
assumed: every field of StockGenerator_Category is private, so a subclass could
neither read them nor reuse its generation. Reproducing the forty lines that
matter is honest; inheriting a class whose state is invisible would be a subclass
in name only. Generation still runs through Core's own
StockGeneratorUtility.TryMakeForStock, so stuff, quality and stacking behave
exactly as they do for every vanilla trader.

Called in through Core's own passing-ship manager from the designated gate
console, so the trade window, the beacon rule and the delivery are all vanilla.
No new building, no new bench and no new UI window: a gizmo and a float menu. A
locked row says which lock is holding it, because with three independent
conditions "you cannot do this" is a useless message. All three are re-checked at
the moment of unlocking rather than trusting that the menu which offered the
button still reflects reality. A tier naming a research project that is not
loaded stays shut, because saying unavailable is honest and opening a gate
because its key is missing is not.

The project's own checkers earned their place again. Two of the three new def
files did not parse, because their comments contained a double hyphen, which XML
forbids inside a comment -- and the compiler was perfectly happy, and the build
succeeded. check-dlc-gating and check-package-integrity both caught it. The
parser's own message says only "not well-formed (invalid token)" at a column,
which says nothing about the rule, and prose naturally wants an em-dash typed
that way, so check-package-integrity gained a targeted check naming the rule and
the line. Sanity-tested by planting a bad comment, confirming the failure, and
removing it.
