Grand spaces, pillars, and the Backrooms stop caving in (0.12.49-dev)

Stage one of the coordinate rebuild. A Backrooms level was 60x60 with a
hard-coded 3x3 grid of eight slots at 19-cell spacing, producing 6 to 8
rooms of 10 to 16 cells. Every one of those numbers was a constant.

Owner direction: "and everything doesnt have to be square rooms and
rectangle halways and u can use walls as pillars making the 0 level rooms be
grand large spaces and leas than 60-100 romms and this can propigate depper".
That revised a 60-to-100-room warren answered an hour earlier, and it is the
better call: a hall eighty cells across cannot fit in a 19-cell slot, so the
room count going down is what pays for the rooms getting grand.

Slots, spacing, room span and room count are all functions of depth now.
Depth 1 is six halls of 80x80 with 144 pillars each; depth 6 is 42 rooms of
24. Verified at every depth: nothing lands off the map, every span is even so
CellRect.CenterCell stays on the slot centre where doors and corridors are
aimed, and there are always ten cells of rock between neighbours for a
corridor to carve. The shape is a serpentine chain through the slot grid, so
consecutive rooms are always grid neighbours and the chain is connected
without a search.

The pillar lattice sits at 6 against Core's RoofMaxSupportDistance of 6.9,
never on the centre cross, narrowest free run five cells. It is decided in
RoomLayoutPlanner.PillarCells and nowhere else, because the planner has to
prove a room is still walkable with the pillars in it before any map exists,
and two independent derivations of one rule is the defect that stopped every
coordinate generating for thirty-nine checkpoints one checkpoint ago.

The Backrooms do not cave in, and only the Backrooms. Owner direction:
"backrooms can not and shall not have cave ins so removing walls floors
columns shall not cause mountain overhead to column collapse", scoped the
same minute to "tgis is only for backrooms".

The existing code was wrong about this and its comment hid how wrong.
BackroomsContainment argued from VanishOnCollapse => !isThickRoof that a
coordinate could be mined to nothing and never open a hole, and treated the
collapse itself as acceptable. Half right: no hole opens, but Core gates
every cave-in on RoofDef.canCollapse, which defaults to true and which Core
sets false on none of its three roofs, so thick roof still drops
CollapsedRocks and crushes what stands beneath.

Fixed with a roof def of our own, RR_RoofBackroomsOverhead, canCollapse
false, isThickRoof true. Patching RoofRockThick was considered and refused:
it would stop mountains collapsing in every colony for every mod in the
profile. A RoofDef carries no texture, graphic or audio we author, so this
stays inside the existing-content-only rule, and every Core behaviour that
matters reads isThickRoof rather than the def's identity.

FillWithRock now suspends the region and room updater for the duration, which
is what Core's own GenStep_RocksFromGrid does and for the same reason. At
60x60 that was 3,600 cells; at 300x300 it is up to 90,000. Restored in a
finally so a throw mid-fill cannot leave region updates switched off.

A coordinate's geometry had no proof coverage at all, and that is measured
rather than asserted: every constant in RoomLayoutPlanner was changed and all
forty existing proofs still passed. proof-coordinate-layout.py is the 41st
proof and parses the constants out of the C# rather than hard-coding them.

A plant then found a hole in that design. Because the model copies the
formulas while parsing the constants, deleting an algorithm step is invisible
to it: removing "if (span % 2 != 0) { span--; }" walked straight past the
computed evenness claim. Every modelled formula is now paired with a source
claim. Two of the plants were themselves wrong rather than the proof being
wrong, which is worth recording: Margin = 1 does not push rooms off the map,
because the spacing grows as the margin shrinks and the arithmetic
self-corrects. plant-coordinate-layout.py is the 12th suite, 34 of 34.

proof-interior-resource.py also objected correctly to the roof change, having
asserted RoofDefOf.RoofRockThick by name. It asserts the property now: thick,
so no sky, and non-collapsing, so no cave-in.

This needs a fresh save, by owner decision: one shape, the 60x60 path
dropped, PlannerVersion 3.

Held for stage two on purpose. The owner chose 4-6 onward gates and
MaximumNaturalDepth 3 to 6, then superseded an LRU-eviction answer with a
hard map cap and no eviction, which is better on every count: nothing the
player looted or built ever resets, memory is bounded by construction, and
the limit is diegetic. Their clarification grounds it in Core -
Prefs.MaxNumberOfPlayerSettlements is a 1-to-5 player slider that Core
enforces for settlements but cannot see a coordinate map. The cap and the
raised gate count ship together, because either alone is wrong.
