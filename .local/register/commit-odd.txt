Odd origin: what comes out of the Backrooms is marked (0.7.2-dev)

The owner's own design, given as the answer to a narrow question about floors and
going a long way past it:

    we can add a flag to item from the back rooms like (odd) or something like
    that and have quests and missions and contracts and stuff for like 1000
    (odd) cotton or like 10 uninstalled electic stoves(odd) ... that can give
    reason for the players to have to advance and excplore and haul and use the
    spaces iin the backrooms

Why it is worth building first. Thirty-one cross-map work families have been
moving real goods through a gate since 0.5.0, and nothing in the game has ever
asked for those goods by where they came from. A contract demanding odd cotton
cannot be filled from the colony's own fields at any price, only by going in,
working the space and hauling it out. That converts the existing work engine into
an economy and gives ordinary Core resources a second tier of value without
inventing a single item, texture or resource, which keeps it inside the
content-reuse policy rather than being an exception to it.

Three laundering routes existed and all three are closed before shipping, because
the mark is the thing a player would most like to forge.

A marked stack must never merge with an unmarked one, and the danger runs both
ways: odd absorbing ordinary would manufacture odd goods out of colony stock and
defeat every contract at once, while ordinary absorbing odd would destroy goods
somebody crossed a gate to fetch. Core has the exact hook and it is used rather
than worked around -- ThingWithComps.CanStackWith consults every comp's
AllowStackWith and gates TryAbsorbStack, verified in source. Splitting is the
second route, closed by PostSplitOff carrying the mark onto the piece.

The third is the design one. Marking anything that spawns on a Backrooms map
would be an open route: haul a thousand ordinary cotton in, drop it, pick it up,
walk out with a thousand odd cotton. So exactly one place applies a mark -- once,
at the end of generation, before the map can be reached. That is the whole
security model and any future code that marks a thing elsewhere reopens it.

The owner's own example is an uninstalled stove rather than a resource.
Uninstalling wraps the building in a MinifiedThing whose InnerThing is the
original, so the comp and its saved flag travel untouched. What that costs is
that every read has to look through the wrapper, since asking a MinifiedThing
directly reports every uninstalled stove as ordinary.

The marker is appended to ThingDef.comps at startup rather than by an XML patch.
The direction is deliberately open-ended -- "for all things materials and
resources ect ect" -- so it has to reach every carryable thing in the loaded
game, including items from the other 274 mods. PatchOperationAdd can only append
to a comps node that already exists, and most item defs have none, so a patch
would have applied to an arbitrary subset and failed silently on the rest.
Appending at startup is purely additive: no def replaced, nothing removed, no
other mod's files touched. Two filters keep it honest -- the def must actually
instantiate comps, because a thingClass that is not a ThingWithComps never builds
its comp list and the marker would be dead weight that looks like it works; and
the thing must be carryable out, meaning an item or an uninstallable building.

Four Core hooks carry the whole feature with no Harmony and no patch to a Core
method: AllowStackWith, PostSplitOff, TransformLabel and PostExposeData. The
"(odd)" is a keyed string, so the word itself stays a translation decision.

Not covered, and named in TODO.md rather than deferred: the contracts that will
demand odd goods, and materials recovered by deconstructing a marked building --
uninstall preserves the mark because the building survives, deconstruct destroys
it and spawns fresh resources through GenLeaving, which exposes no public hook.
