Four answers, and a tier 0 unlock that did nothing (0.12.4-dev)

Four open questions closed, and a real defect the first of them uncovered.

THE FIND. Wiring reserveChargePowerWatts meant reading what already consumed
the gate's power values, and something did not:

    public float MinimumPowerHeadroomWatts   // applies RR_Cap_ReserveDiscipline
    { get { ... } }                          // and was itself READ BY NOTHING

RR_Cap_ReserveDiscipline is the tier 0 Facilities unlock and its entire effect
was to lower that number. So the card promised "the gate needs less spare
headroom above its draw before it will open" and changed nothing a player
could ever observe.

That is invariant 136 exactly, and exactly why proof-research-branches.py
cannot catch it: the capability WAS read, by real code, and the code reading it
was itself dead. A live read site is not a live effect.

So the sweep became a general proof. proof-live-effects.py walks every public
property on the gate comp whose body reads GateProps - fifteen of them - and
insists each is consulted elsewhere. It found TWO MORE:
EmergencyReturnCostWattDays and RecoveryOpeningCostWattDays, which are dead
ACCESSORS rather than dead values. The readout showed the totals that include
them and never showed how much of the total was the way home, which is the
number that decides whether it is safe to send anybody. It has its own line
now. Three dead members, one sweep, and the sweep keeps working after everybody
has forgotten why it exists.

reserveChargePowerWatts = "A supply requirement before opening". The circuit
must be GENERATING this much, not holding it: a battery is a buffer, not
supply, and a gate opened on one charged battery and no generator is a gate
about to strand a crew. NativeGenerationWatts sums only what is actually
producing. Plus margin above what the gate already draws, which is what makes
the tier 0 unlock real. Two distinct refusals, because "cannot deliver enough"
and "no margin above the draw" are different problems with different fixes.

It gates OPENING only, deliberately not NativeBindingFailureKey, which is read
every tick - a generation dip there would emergency-return a crew already
across, and the chart's rule is that a lapse blocks the next opening, never the
current one. The proof asserts the absence.

Natural gates became informed consent rather than prohibition, on the owner's
own relaxation: "dont worry about it, can we at least do a rim style pop up
warning". It is also the only honest option, because Core decides
destructibility at the def level and changing it would make every door in every
colony indestructible for every player and every other mod. A player may close
their own way in; they may not do it by accident. A laboratory gate gets no
warning - it is a machine they built and warning about ordinary construction is
how a player learns to click through warnings. A bounded map sweep rather than
a comp ticking on every door in the colony.

Solo/group guidance is option three: no request line until contact, for honesty
rather than difficulty. Nobody is helping them because nobody knows they exist,
and a quest-giver would have to be invented. Instead four hints in the
survivors' own voice, each once, on conditions already true when they fire.
None is an objective; nothing tracks whether the player listened. A hint that
checked whether you obeyed it would be a quest wearing a costume.

Idle draw: confirmed at 250 W. No code needed - the shipped behaviour is the
intended behaviour, and the open balance question from 0.11.5-dev is settled.

TWO PROOF MISTAKES OF MY OWN. The declaration scan matched nothing, because a
property body is `{ get { ... } }` and the regex had a brace-nesting limit - so
it reported zero exposed properties and PASSED every per-property claim by
having none to check. That is invariant 152, written this same session.
Rewritten so it cannot silently match zero. And the hints built keyed strings
at runtime; check-keyed-strings refused it and was right, because a constructed
key cannot be verified in either direction and a typo ships as a raw key on
screen.

Fault-planted three ways, including restoring the original bug, and correct on
every one.
