The company clears the site, and the mod declares what it needs (0.12.76-dev)

When everyone at the laboratory goes down, The Company arrives. Not when they
die - when they are all down. The squad leaves nobody standing who was there,
buries the dead where the ground will take a grave and burns them where it will
not, repairs the damage, shuts every connection at the gate, takes every bond on
every map, draws up to 25,000,000 for restocking the petty cash, and leaves three
people and a crate. The player never loses the game.

The owner's first question found two false statements in my own handoff. "are u
shure zero goon squad code is written weve gone over this before by a different
name" - I had written ZERO on the strength of src/ matching the last checkpoint,
which proves only that this session wrote none. proof-facility-relief.py is proof
FIVE and two of its claims contradicted the direction. And Pyre is Ideology, not
Core: that check ran against the installed game instead of against Core, and for
a Core-only mod that is the whole distinction. It read as a pass because it
answered the wrong question.

The owner's own scoping then saved both of those claims. "this only happens for
the lab secnerio for now" means the squad is a separate path, forking above the
relief's trigger, so the store and solo branches keep five staff and a death-only
trigger. Both old claims hold untouched and the comment carrying the old reasoning
is scoped rather than deleted - it is still true about the path it guards.

The grave does not fit indoors, and that was measured rather than discovered at
runtime. Core's Grave is (1,2) and needs Diggable; ten Core terrains carry it and
every constructed floor is excluded. So burial goes to open ground and the
fallback is destruction. No crematorium is built: a bill needs a worker and
nobody is alive to work it.

The mod now declares 294 hard dependencies - five expansions and 289 mods, each
with a name and a link - plus 295 loadAfter entries, generated from the owner's
own ModsConfig.xml read-only, with a cycle check over every active mod's four
load-order tags. Declaring them exposed a defect nobody was looking for: the
package sat at position 197 of 296, with 99 mods loading after it, and a patch
cannot see a def from a mod that loads later.

check-register-compliance.py refused all of it with "the package must load and run
against Core alone". The checker was not defective; its premise was, and the
register is guidance rather than law by the owner's own standing correction. A
prohibition became an assertion instead of being deleted. check-dlc-gating.py's
rule survives and matters more, because MayRequire is the same graceful guard in
XML that GetNamedSilentFail is in C# - only its stated reason was stale, and a
reason nobody believes is worse than no reason.

Then the sixth instance of an instrument reading its own prose, and the first that
was mine: the fix script searched its result for the phrase it had removed, and

had landed; the verification was the defect. A second of the same family followed
