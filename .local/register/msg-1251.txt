Letting a place go, so a machine gate can aim deeper (0.12.51-dev)

Owner question: "yeah so if the player discovers and goes through a natural
gate how do they turn them off to use the machine gates for more controll and
aiming deeper?", and the trap named plainly: "get 5 natural gates u cant use
a machine gate". Asked how, they chose an Operations held-places list with a
Release button.

A correction is owed first, and it makes the trap worse than the previous
record said. That record claimed discovering a gate was free because no map
existed until somebody crossed. It is wrong.
NaturalFrontierService.Discover calls
PortalAddressService.RegisterNaturalAddress, which calls
DestinationService.EnsureSite immediately, because a natural edge is
registered against the far side's own ReturnAnchor and that Thing does not
exist until the map does. A discovery costs a slot at the moment it is made.
So the budget check in Discover is load-bearing rather than over-eager, and
five discoveries really do lock a player out of their own machine gates,
exactly as described. That is why this shipped now instead of next
checkpoint. The prior record is annotated rather than rewritten.

A natural gate is permanently open and is never closed. What a release lets
go of is the space behind it. The door stays: still there, still marked, and
remembering which place it led to on its own comp.

The pairing has to move before the edge is removed. The edge is what records
that this door goes to that coordinate, and every endpoint of it lives on the
map about to be torn down, so the edge must go - which makes it the only
other record of the pairing. Remove it first and the door becomes an ordinary
marked door with the place behind it unreachable for ever. So the release
writes the coordinate id onto the door's comp while the edge still says so.

The order is the whole of the safety, and the proof asserts it as an ordering
by index rather than as the presence of three calls: doors told where they
led, then edges removed, then the map and world object torn down. Reverse
either pair and a record points at something that no longer exists.

releasedByPlayer exists to answer one question that cannot otherwise be
answered. EnsureSite rightly refuses to rebuild a coordinate that has no site
but whose rooms were surveyed, because a broken reference must not replace an
already explored graph. A deliberate release is indistinguishable from that
unless it says so. The exemption covers that and nothing else: a competing
owner or a live map still refuses with the flag set, and the flag is cleared
the moment the place is generated again, because an exemption that outlives
its reason is a hole.

The coordinate record and its rooms are kept, so re-opening returns to the
same place - the same rooms in the same shape. Its contents are not, because
the interior regenerates from the seed. That is the honest price of not
holding it open, and it is why this is a deliberate action with a red
confirmation that counts the items first rather than the eviction timer the
owner considered and rejected. A release that discarded the graph would hand
the player a different place back, which is worse than either.

Refusals name themselves: the headquarters, crew inside, a crossing in
flight, or a place with no live map. A prisoner or an animal counts as
somebody inside, because a released map takes its contents with it.

Re-opening is a gizmo on the door that led there, calling
RegisterNaturalAddress - the same path that created the place, so there is no
second implementation to drift. It is disabled rather than hidden at the
budget, because a player at their limit needs to see what they are at the
limit of, and it only forgets the shelved place on success: forgetting on
failure would strand it for ever over a transient refusal.

The pane lists the colonies too, because they are the other half of the
budget and a player counting slots needs to see why three of five are gone
before they blame the Backrooms. Neither outcome of pressing Release is
silent.
