Plan the public face: site, Workshop page, collection

Owner direction, recorded verbatim and explicitly future-tense: a
GitHub Pages site cataloguing the whole mod at a real domain rather
than a github.io path, then the Steam Workshop mod page, then the
collection, with write-ups linking to each other and to the site.

Written up as docs/PUBLIC_RELEASE_PLAN.md rather than left as a queue
entry, because the ordering the owner named is a real dependency chain
and the parts that are the owner's to decide need naming now rather
than being discovered later.

Why it is last: every artefact in it describes the mod, so each is
written twice if the mod is still changing underneath. README.md
already announced a version twenty-five checkpoints out of date on the
front page until a checker caught it, and a published site and a
Workshop listing are the same failure with an audience.

The ordering: the mod settles, then the site deploys and is reachable,
then the Workshop page whose write-up links to it, then the collection
whose write-up links to both. Publishing a link to a page that does
not exist yet is the one mistake strangers can see.

The site must be generated rather than hand-maintained.
tools/make-readable-html.py is already the seed of it, and a
hand-written site would be a fourth copy of the truth that drifts from
the other three. check-doc-conformance.py should cover the generated
output once that output exists.

Three decisions are the owner's and are named rather than assumed:
which domain and whether it is registered, since a custom domain and a
project-path deploy generate different internal links; which branch
Pages publishes from, given the four-branch cascade; and whether to
drive Steam with Playwright at all.

That last one is recorded as the owner's idea and deliberately not
treated as approval. It means operating an authenticated session on
the owner's Steam account, which is a different class of action from
anything done here so far, and it needs explicit permission with a
stated scope. The alternative costs the owner very little: a prepared
write-up in Steam's own markup, ready to paste, plus a checklist of
the settings to set.

All six checkers pass. No new def, asset, patch operation or work
type. No game launched, no test run, no RimSort profile touched.
