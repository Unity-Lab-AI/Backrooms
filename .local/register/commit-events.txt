Things that happen, and the echo reaching items (0.8.4-dev)

The events half of "wild waky carzxzy creepy things when u add places and
events". The places shipped four checkpoints ago; events had been named as
outstanding ever since rather than quietly dropped.

Seven of them, and every one has an answer the player can actually perform,
because the frozen threat rules demand one: readable warning, learnable rule, at
least one countermeasure, no unavoidable instant failure.

The lights case is the best of them. CompFlickable.SwitchIsOn has a public
setter, so the event switches every light off using vanilla's own flick switch --
which means the countermeasure is vanilla too and the player already knows how to
perform it. That is a far better answer than a bespoke darkness mechanic. Cold is
answered by clothing, a heater or leaving. Seepage is answered by the cleaning
family, which already crosses a gate. Rearranged things are moved and never
destroyed. Presence does nothing at all, and exists so that a quiet band still
has texture.

Nothing here can trap anybody. No effect damages a pawn, destroys a thing or
blocks a route, and the threshold room is excluded from every effect, always --
that is the no-unavoidable-instant-failure rule made concrete rather than
promised, because whatever happens, walking back out is still possible.

Rearrangement moves loose items only and never fixtures, because the clue system
records a landmark per room and moving one would quietly break a trail a player
is following. It refuses to move bonds, since a player's money is not scenery,
and a failed move puts the item back rather than leaving it unspawned.

Bounded and non-replaying: at most two events per visit, and a non-repeatable one
is recorded on the coordinate so a revisit resumes rather than replays. A space a
player knows should not perform its party trick every single time they walk in;
that turns an unsettling event into a chore.

The construction echo now reaches items and equipment, following the direction
that arrived mid-build. Production benches were already covered as buildings, but
items needed a different test and getting it right mattered: faction ownership
cannot work for them, because a stack of steel in a colony stockpile has a null
faction exactly like one lying in a Backrooms corridor. Origin is the right test
instead and it already existed -- anything stamped Outside came into existence
somewhere the player was, which excludes a coordinate's own contents by the same
stroke and stops the place echoing its own things back at itself. Bonds are never
echoed.

The comment-dash checker earned itself back. Two of the new def files failed to
parse because of a double hyphen inside an XML comment, and the check added in
0.7.6-dev caught it and named the rule and the line rather than leaving the
parser's useless "not well-formed (invalid token)" at a column. That is the
second time this exact trap has been hit since the check was written, and the
first time it cost nothing to diagnose.
