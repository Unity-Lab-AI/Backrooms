The place copies your people, and holds them until you find them (0.8.5-dev)

Items and production benches echoed in the previous checkpoint. This is the other
half of that direction: echoes of the branch's inhabitants, which is a different
thing entirely from echoing objects.

An echo carries the name and the clothes of a colonist who is alive and at home
right now. Deliberately not somebody the branch lost -- that is the Missing
family and it is a different and sadder feeling, because a body with a familiar
name is grief. An echo is uncanny, and it is uncanny precisely because the real
one is standing in the base at the same moment the player is looking at this one.

That constraint is enforced rather than assumed. The source must be alive, not
downed, on a player home map, and not on the coordinate map itself -- meeting
your own echo while you are standing there is a sillier effect than meeting it
while you know they are at home. If nobody qualifies, no echo is placed at all,
because a generic stranger under an echo family would be a worse encounter than
none: the whole effect is the recognition.

Only the surface is copied: the name and the apparel, which are what a player
recognises at a glance and are together enough to land. Skills, traits,
backstory, health and relationships are deliberately not copied, because an echo
is something that has seen your colonist rather than something that is them.
Copying the interior would make it a duplicate, which is a weaker and much more
confusing idea, and would hand the player a free second copy of their best worker
if they ever recruited one. Echoes therefore cannot be recruited at all, and the
survivor mark is never applied to them.

Apparel is copied rather than taken. Taking the real clothes would strip a living
colonist from across a gate, which would be a bug wearing a feature's clothes.
Each piece is made fresh from the same def and stuff, so quality and damage
differ, which is fitting for a likeness rather than a duplicate. Whatever the
generator already dressed the echo in is removed first, or it wears two shirts
and reads as a mess rather than as a copy.

An echo is never hostile. Hostility would break the warning-first rule outright,
since a thing wearing a friendly name that attacks is the definition of an
unreadable threat, and the existing ConfigErrors guard already refuses any
hostile family that is not Psychotic.

Determinism needed a specific fix. Candidates are sorted by thingIDNumber rather
than taken in list order, because list order varies with spawn and load order:
without the sort the same seed would echo a different colonist after a reload,
and the entire effect depends on it being the same person every time.

The owner caught a real defect in this mid-build and it is fixed in the same
checkpoint rather than shipped: Backrooms inhabitants would die off if a player
explored slowly. Everything placed in a coordinate is a live pawn on a live map,
so needs tick from the moment it exists, and a survivor three rooms away would
starve before a cautious player ever reached them. The rescue would have been
impossible for exactly the player most likely to want it, and it would have read
as a broken feature rather than as somebody having died. The same applied to a
hostile freezing in a cold band's coordinate and to a body rotting behind a door
nobody had opened.

Fog of war is the right discovery signal and the owner named it. RimWorld already
tracks per cell whether the player has seen it, so nothing had to be invented,
saved or kept in sync, and it is already the exact question a player experiences
as having been somewhere yet. Needs are topped back up on a bounded sweep rather
than frozen, because stopping them ticking would need Harmony and the observable
result is identical. Malnutrition, hypothermia and heatstroke are cleared and
corpse rot is held at zero, which is safe precisely because this only ever runs
on somebody nobody has seen: no player decision is undone and nothing observable
is reversed. Mood is deliberately left alone, because a held pawn is being kept
alive rather than made happy, and a forced mood would produce somebody who reads
as uncannily content in a place designed to be unbearable. Discovery starts their
clock.
