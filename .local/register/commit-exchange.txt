The exchange, and credits back out as paper (0.7.7-dev)

Selling physical valuables to the parent corporation for credits, and drawing
credits back out as bonds. This closes the money loop: credits now have a source,
a physical form, a store and a sink.

The rate is the whole design. Odd goods go at one and a half times market, which
is the owner's answer and is justified on its own terms: the corporation cannot
source them anywhere else, and the player paid for them in gate time, power and
risk rather than in silver. Ordinary valuables go at eighty-five percent,
because the company is instant with no trader, no caravan and no travel, and it
takes a cut for that.

Traders therefore stay the better price for ordinary goods if you are willing to
wait for one, and that is deliberate rather than an oversight. A company paying
full market would make every trader who buys valuables pointless and would make
the convenience free. Paying above market for odd and below for ordinary is what
keeps the two economies pulling in different directions instead of one swallowing
the other. It also finally gives gold and silver a job: until now a vault of gold
was dead weight until a trader turned up wanting it.

Value comes from each thing's own MarketValue, so the exchange tracks the game's
economy and any mod that reprices anything, rather than a table this mod would
have to maintain against 294 others.

The exchange runs on a designated credit beacon's radius and takes everything
tradeable inside it, because that contract already exists: a Core trade beacon
already means that what is in the circle is what is on the table. It borrows a
mental model the player has rather than inventing selection rules, and the radius
is the control. The gizmo shows the split before committing -- item count, total,
and how much of it is the odd premium.

Bonds in range are never sold. A bond is already credits, banking one is a
different action with a different meaning, and quietly selling a million-credit
bond at eighty-five percent would be a way to destroy a player's money by
accident. Pawns and corpses are excluded outright. Everything is valued first,
then destroyed, then posted as a single transaction, which is the same shape bond
banking uses so that a reload mid-sale cannot credit a subset twice.

For withdrawal the denomination ladder is the menu, filtered to what the account
can cover and ordered largest first. There is no number to type, no slider, and
no way to ask for an amount that cannot be represented: pick a rung, get one bond
of that size. That is honest about what is happening, because the corporation
prints a specific instrument rather than dispensing change. Withdrawing a larger
sum is what IssueBonds is for, and it still pays out largest-first.

The loop now runs end to end. Cross a gate and haul things out, and they are odd.
An odd-supply contract wants them by origin and cannot be filled from your own
fields, or sell them at the beacon above market because nobody else has them.
Credits land in the account. Draw them as bonds, store them in a vault, lose them
in a fire. Spend them on catalogue access, which decides what the corporation
will sell you, which makes the next coordinate survivable -- and the pressure
system means you need ordinary material down there to work at all.
