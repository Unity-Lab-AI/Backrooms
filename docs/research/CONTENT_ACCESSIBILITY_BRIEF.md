# Content and accessibility brief

## Content direction

The project targets mature psychological horror and company management. The owner selected the strongest horror, body-horror, captivity, and distressing-event presentation the game and tested profile can support. Content must still be communicated through RimWorld-scale simulation and readable feedback, not require bespoke cinematic scenes.

Kane Pixels' Backrooms series is the primary lore reference; the A24 feature is reviewed separately. Adapt the source indirectly rather than recreating specific scenes or characters. The company, staff, dialogue, cases, equipment, and mission systems are original game design. Broader community canon is outside shipped content unless the owner expands the scope.

Expected content themes include:

- hazardous exploration, injuries, death, missing-person cases, and uncertain returns;
- psychological distress, isolation, spatial disorientation, reality distortion, and disturbing imagery;
- non-consensual detention/custody as a management decision, interviews/interrogation as a consequential system, and escape or rescue risks;
- body horror and hostile entities presented through the game engine's available visual and text systems;
- corporate exploitation, financial pressure, staff disagreement, and ethically difficult operational choices.

Do not market the content as “all ages.” Complete the distribution platform's then-current content questionnaire honestly before a public release; this brief does not assign an ESRB, PEGI, or other formal rating.

## Accessibility requirements

- Convey every essential danger, objective, status, and failure using at least one persistent text/icon/state cue that does not depend on color alone.
- Caption or provide readable text equivalents for radio messages, alarms, and entity cues. Keep transcripts and logs available after a temporary message disappears.
- Avoid essential instructions that require hearing a sound, noticing a brief flash, interpreting fast camera movement, or reacting within an unannounced short window.
- Where technically supported by RimWorld, provide settings to reduce flashing, screen shake, intense visual effects, and repeated audio; preserve the gameplay warning through a quieter equivalent cue.
- Test custom company screens at multiple UI scales and with longer-than-English strings. Keep the active objective, action, preconditions, consequence, and recovery path visible without relying on hover-only information.
- Preserve vanilla hotkeys and keyboard navigation. Custom controls need clear focus order, input validation, and a route usable without a mouse where the engine permits.
- Make difficult content systems legible before commitment: explain custody duration, risk, consent/escape consequences, injury exposure, and financial effects in the relevant interface.

## Acceptance checks

- 100% of player-facing text is keyed for localization; a long-string test locale has no clipped critical controls.
- All critical warnings remain available as text and non-color status cues after the initial event.
- A player can finish the tutorial and first expedition without needing sound or color discrimination.
- Reduced-effect settings preserve warning and counterplay information; verify each setting in the release profile.
- Record the current platform questionnaire and content declarations with the release evidence; do not invent a formal age-rating label.
