# Kane Pixels Backrooms Series — Fan Cliff Notes

**Research method:** fan-maintained episode pages in the [Kane Pixels Backrooms Wiki](https://kane-pixels-backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Backrooms_(series)), read 2026-09-27. These are short paraphrases of a community source, not direct reviews of the videos or creator-confirmed canon. The project's selected [official YouTube playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLVAh-MgDVqvDUEq6qDXqORBioE4Yhol_z) remains the 23-entry index; its order is not the story chronology.

The fan guide's broad story arc begins with a young filmmaker falling into the Backrooms and encountering an unknown lifeform. Later entries follow Async's doorway experiments and field teams as the company studies the space; expeditions become more dangerous, records conflict, and missing people and time shifts create lasting consequences. Treat that as a fan overview, not a definitive explanation of every event.

## Fan-suggested story order

The community [timeline page](https://kane-pixels-backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Timeline) puts the events in a different order from the upload list. This short outline is only a fan-assembled guide; some dates are inferred from labels or related media, and it has not been checked against every official upload.

1. **Before Async's main project:** The timeline places an unusual 1972 event and an early prototype test before the company develops its larger doorway project. Treat the cause and connection between them as unknown.
2. **Async builds and opens the Threshold:** Async's business pitch and repeated experiments lead to a successful opening in 1989. The fan chronology puts the earthquake and opening near the same date, but its own notes warn against claiming the earthquake caused the Threshold.
3. **The first expeditions go wrong:** Late 1989 into early 1990 brings strange survey findings, missing people, an unexplained body, safety rules, lost time, camera anomalies, and a growing outpost. The company learns enough to keep going, but not enough to make the space predictable.
4. **A missing researcher returns:** In May 1990, a returning employee triggers a security crisis. The wiki timeline connects the incident to Async's public-facing presentation, detention and escape, an armed expedition, and a cover story. Staff disagree about what the company should disclose.
5. **The space keeps changing:** Later expeditions encounter sealed routes, altered rooms, retail-like spaces, missing crew, and threats. The notes suggest Async becomes more organized as its people and maps become less dependable.
6. **Personal incursions continue:** The fan chronology places Kane's found-footage trip in 1991, another lone person's recording in 1995, and Ravi's in 1997. These are separate human stories as well as evidence that the phenomenon reaches beyond Async's planned expeditions.

The same fan timeline also inserts Clark, Mary, and events from the A24 feature among the series entries. That placement is a community interpretation, not an official continuity statement. For this project, keep the A24 story note separate from the Kane series until an official source establishes their relationship.
