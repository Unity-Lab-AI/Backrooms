# Beyond the gate

A **coordinate** is an address, and the map it opens onto.

You visit one. You do not settle one — the game will say so if you try to put one on the books.

---

## Depth

Every coordinate is a **300 by 300** map. What changes with depth is how that space is divided.

| Depth | What it is like |
|---|---|
| **1** | Around 36 rooms, large and open, including one grand hall more than twice the size of any other room |
| **2** | Around 49 rooms, smaller |
| **3 and deeper** | Around 60 rooms — a warren, at the ceiling |

The first band is the fewest and largest spaces you will see. Going deeper means more rooms, more
branching corridors, and less open floor.

Rooms too wide for a roof to span unaided carry **pillars**, which is why the largest spaces read as
built rather than drawn.

A few spaces are **sealed**, with no corridor reaching them at all.

## What a room is made of

The shallow rooms are the yellow ones: wood and worn plank, sparse, wrong in the way you expect.

Deeper, the building material changes with depth, and each look covers a whole place rather than
one room:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Poolrooms** | Wet tile and hard light |
| **Machinery** | Plate, grating and grease |
| **Abandoned offices** | Worn carpet under dead strip light |
| **Cold storage** | Concrete and frost |
| **Wrong** | Where the place stops agreeing with itself |

Which one you get follows depth with some variation, and it is fixed per address — a place looks
the same every time you return.

What a room is built from is also salvage: steel walls are steel.

Further in, the furnishing stops pretending too. Rooms are furnished as laboratories, workshops,
nurseries and dormitories. Runs of rooms are furnished as one place rather than three. Corridors
have benches in them.

## Things move

Go back to a space you have been before and something will not be where you left it.

Nothing tells you, and not every time.

---

## Bands

Every coordinate has a **band** — how hostile it currently is.

It rises with depth and with what your colony is worth, and it decides what comes looking for your
crew.

The Atlas pane prints it, so you never have to learn a band by being hurt by it.

## The Atlas

| | |
|---|---|
| **Surveyed rooms** | Appear with what connects to what |
| **Unsurveyed rooms** | Blank |
| **Link list** | Needs route telemetry unlocked first |

---

## What lives there

Wanderers, the missing, the dead, survivors worth bringing home — and things that are none of
those.

Nothing starves or rots before you have found it. Discovery starts their clock, not your exploring
speed.

## What follows you

At the hostile band, an inhabitant will hunt your crew **as far as the threshold**.

Only that far, with one bounded exception: at the deepest band, through an advanced gate, while an
opening is live, something that fits may step through behind them. It then behaves like anything
else that got inside your base.

Closing the connection before it arrives is what stops it.

A gate is never an objective, a lure or a spawn target. Nothing is ever drawn toward one.

## Nothing leaks in

Inhabitants come from this mod's own roster only. No content from any other mod appears in
Backrooms generation.

---

## Bringing something back

The point of a crossing is a record.

| Step | Where |
|---|---|
| 1. Record a route, a distortion and an entity observation | In the field |
| 2. Analyse it | A bound laboratory, by a pawn, over time |
| 3. Have a second person review the report | Operations → Investigation |
| 4. Spend the insight | A company project |

The Investigation pane shows which of the three parts each recording still lacks.

**None of these steps is timed.** A recording waits as long as you need it to.

## Odd goods

Anything carried out of a coordinate is marked **odd** and never stacks with the ordinary kind.

A thousand odd cotton is a thing you went and fetched, not a thing you grew. Buyers turn up wanting
goods by origin and paying well over the ordinary rate.
