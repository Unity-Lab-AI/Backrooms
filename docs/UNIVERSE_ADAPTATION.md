# Universe adaptation notes

## Chosen reference

Per the project owner's direction, Kane Pixels' Backrooms series and the A24 feature are the selected references, adapted indirectly. Do not directly recreate specific scenes or characters. Broader community canon is outside the shipped-content scope; keep any provenance checks against it separate from these references.

The campaign is an original RimWorld company simulation informed by broad themes from the selected references. RimWorld's interplanetary setting and colony management let the company expand beyond an Earth research site. Do not present the campaign as a canonical continuation or direct recreation of the series or feature.

## Lore-to-game translation

| Source element | Campaign treatment |
| --- | --- |
| A-Sync's organized investigation of the space | The player manages a small frontier A-Sync facility: staffing, budgets, labs, security, power, logistics, and field operations. The source institution supplies the research context; the player's facility owns local decisions and consequences. |
| The Threshold as an engineered access point | Build, calibrate, power, secure, and maintain the machine gate. Its operating window, stability, and recall options are gated by technology and preparation. |
| The Complex/A-Space as an enormous environment rather than a normal settlement | Each coordinate is a stable, seeded expedition site made of rooms and routes. The company can map, revisit, and expand into destinations without loading one infinite map. |
| Recorded experiments, field footage, and research documents | Turn recordings, radio calls, equipment telemetry, site notes, interviews, and incident reports into evidence objects and research leads. Present useful facts in short readable excerpts; link the rest to the source log rather than dumping large transcripts into the game. |
| Dangerous tests, missing people, and incomplete returns | Create expedition chains where a team can go missing, make contact, return injured, return with contradictory accounts, or require a rescue mission. Let the player act on evidence before a crew is simply declared lost. |
| Ordinary industrial interiors become uncanny through exact changes | Reuse recognizable room categories, materials, fluorescent lighting, service infrastructure, and furniture as the baseline. Use intentional spatial changes such as a shifted doorway, impossible adjacency, repeated hall, changed room dimensions, or a feature that has moved since the last visit. |
| Human curiosity and personal stakes around the anomaly | Track a staff member's work, relationships, skills, fears, motives, and prior exposure alongside company objectives. A pawn's history should affect the expedition and the facility's response. |
| The corporation wants knowledge and useful space | Make research, transport/storage, salvage, and contracts legitimate company goals, but attach payroll, equipment wear, rescue costs, contamination, political pressure, and security liabilities to extraction. |

## Starting campaign frame

The default **Async Industries** start puts the player in charge of a small research/security facility with an unfinished Threshold, limited infrastructure, starter stock, and a handful of staff. Its first milestone is to make the machine operable, verify the safety procedure, and bring a crew back with evidence. The company later funds labs, gate equipment, longer openings, radio stations, supply points, and outposts.

Two alternate starts use the same setting and common progression systems: a furniture-and-knickknack shop with an anomalous basement opening, and a lone survivor already trapped inside a seeded coordinate. The store start adapts A24's official furniture-showroom threshold premise into an original management opening. The survivor start delays company infrastructure until escape, rescue, or an outpost foothold. See the canonical [scenario contract](SCENARIOS.md), the [player-facing scenario overview](GAME_DESIGN.md#scenario-framework-and-opening-scenarios), and the [AI build handoff](AI_BUILD_HANDOFF.md).

RimWorld's colony simulation remains the engine: pawns need food, sleep, recreation, medical care, safe rooms, work assignments, and social stability whether the scenario begins with researchers, shop staff, or one isolated survivor.

## Progression translated from the source premise

1. **Access:** assemble and calibrate one gate; open one short, low-information destination.
2. **Measurement:** develop sensors, recording gear, sample handling, route notes, and return procedures.
3. **Control:** extend opening time; make recall, tethering, communication, and quarantine more dependable.
4. **Commercial operation:** take retrieval and survey contracts, recover furnishings and equipment, staff labs, and order scheduled shipments.
5. **Remote reach:** establish relay stations and outposts, revisit saved coordinates, and respond to openings in settlements.
6. **Industrial expansion:** integrate vehicles, gravships, and later space travel as RimWorld logistics systems supporting a larger research network.
7. **Deep-space study:** combine known room and threat rules into more complex destinations, with rare phenomena that challenge established return methods.

## Content structure for later research files

For each video or film segment, record:

- Source title, creator, URL, publication date, and timestamps reviewed.
- What the audience is shown versus what characters know at that point.
- Spatial rule or anomaly, including whether it is stable, shifting, or uncertain.
- Organization, tools, staffing, communication, containment, and mission objective.
- Human choices, consequences, missing persons, and changes in relationships or behavior.
- Entity or hazard behavior and any evidence/countermeasure revealed.
- Candidate RimWorld implementation: building, work task, quest, map generator tag, item, skill, incident, research, or staff event.
- Which details are direct source references, which are project inventions, and which need a rights/source check before inclusion.
